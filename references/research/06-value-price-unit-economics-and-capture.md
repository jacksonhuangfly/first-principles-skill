# Value, Price, Unit Economics, And Capture

## Related Thinkers

Elon Musk, Peter Drucker, Michael Porter

## Why This File Exists

Ground reasoning about value creation, willingness to pay, and economic capture so the skill does not confuse usage, praise, cost, and profit.

## Source Anchors

Evidence status: editorial synthesis with related background sources; no matching source by Drucker or Porter is retained.

- [Christensen Institute, Jobs to Be Done explainer](../sources/articles/jtbd-source-card.md) supplies reported examples of customer progress and purchase decisions, not a general pricing or willingness-to-pay model.
- [Theory of Constraints explainer](../sources/articles/goldratt-bottleneck-source-card.md), under Throughput Accounting, distinguishes throughput, inventory, and operating expense. It is secondary background, not a validation of this file's subscription unit-economics model.
- The Musk connection and the formulas or cost assumptions used in a live analysis require their own evidence. Use current product data for margins, acquisition costs, and cost floors.

## Distinctions That Matter

- value created versus value captured
- price versus cost floor
- unit health versus aggregate story
- economic signal versus vanity growth

## Canonical Questions

- What concrete outcome creates value for the user?
- What evidence suggests willingness to pay rather than willingness to praise?
- Which unit-level costs are structural and which are self-inflicted?
- What happens to economics once servicing, churn, and recovery work are counted?

## What This Changes In The Skill

- It sharpens the difference between value creation and value capture.
- It keeps pricing questions tied to economics, alternatives, and willingness to pay.
- It helps the skill reason about margin quality rather than raw top-line enthusiasm.

## Good Moves

- connect value to observable consequences for the user
- treat willingness to pay as revealed by behavior under constraint
- force unit economics before believing the aggregate story
- distinguish price optimization from fundamental economic redesign

## Common Misuse

- treating compliments as pricing evidence
- talking about margin without a cost stack
- using top-line growth to hide weak contribution economics
- confusing temporarily subsidized adoption with sustainable value capture

## Example Translation

A $49 subscription is not economically healthy because it sounds reasonable. The real test is whether compute, support, acquisition, and churn recovery still leave a useful contribution margin at the unit level.

## Import Into The Skill

Use this file when the user asks about pricing, costs, margins, monetization, or whether a system is economically coherent.

## Notes

- Load this when revenue, pricing, or margin questions are being answered with vibes or simplistic premium language.
- It is useful whenever product value and business capture are being confused.

## Tension With Neighboring Traditions

- Pricing theory without unit economics can flatter weak businesses.
- Unit economics without user value understanding can optimize the wrong model.

## Mini Cases

- A business with strong top-line growth hides that its best-seeming customers are structurally unprofitable.
- A product is called premium even though willingness to pay is propped up by temporary subsidy or hype.
- A price debate resolves once the team distinguishes user value, market comparison, and irreducible cost floor.

## Diagnostic Prompts

- "What user value is actually being created, and what fraction can be captured?"
- "Which price assumption fails once willingness to pay is compared to alternatives?"
- "What cost or effort is the buyer already paying today?"
- "Where is gross margin flattering us because hidden service cost is missing?"
- "What unit should discipline this economics question?"
- "How would price change if we measured value at the moment the user gets the result?"
- "Which part of the current offer creates value and which part merely decorates it?"
- "Where is the business confusing monetization logic with actual customer value?"

- Separating gross margin from support-heavy economics.

## Translation Patterns

- Turn pricing debate into value and alternative analysis.
- Turn revenue growth into contribution quality.
- Turn “premium” into why people pay above baseline.

- Turn product love into willingness-to-pay evidence.

## Failure Signals

- The answer still treats price as a branding choice detached from economics.
- Value is asserted but no alternative or baseline is named.
- Capture is discussed without contribution quality.
- Hidden service or exception cost is still offstage.
- The product seems valuable but the analysis cannot explain why people do or do not pay.
- The unit of analysis remains unstable.

## What To Pull Forward Operationally

- One cleaner value statement tied to the buyer’s real alternative.
- One unit that should govern the economics conversation.
- One hidden cost that changes the margin story.
- One price or packaging assumption worth testing.
- One distinction between value creation and value capture the current framing blurs.
