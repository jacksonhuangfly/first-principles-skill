# First Causes, Definitions, And Explanation

## Related Thinkers

Aristotle, Euclid, Richard Feynman

## Why This File Exists

Ground first-principles reasoning in first causes, clean definitions, explanatory depth, and the refusal to confuse elegant words with real understanding.

## Source Anchors

Evidence status: partial source support; the operational questions below are editorial synthesis.

- [Aristotle, *Metaphysics*, Book I excerpt](../sources/books/aristotle-first-causes-source-card.md) supports discussion of explanation through causes and first principles. The retained text includes the opening of Part 3; it is a truncated excerpt, not the complete work.
- [Feynman, *Cargo Cult Science*](../sources/transcripts/feynman-cargo-cult.md) supports testing explanations against observations and reporting contrary evidence. It does not establish the separate teaching-style claims associated with his lectures.
- No Euclid text is retained. The connection to definitions and axioms requires further verification before attribution.

## Distinctions That Matter

- definition versus conclusion
- primitive versus derived claim
- explanation versus redescription
- understanding versus verbal fluency

## Canonical Questions

- What is primitive here and what is inherited language?
- Which large noun needs definition before analysis?
- What would count as a real explanation rather than a relabeling?
- Can the claim survive a translation into plainer, more observable language?

## What This Changes In The Skill

- It forces big nouns to earn their keep through definition and decomposition.
- It sharpens the difference between explanation, relabeling, and rhetorical fluency.
- It gives the skill a cleaner starting point for abstract or overloaded questions.

## Good Moves

- ask for the first cause or primitive before following the narrative upward
- make overloaded concepts legible by splitting them into dimensions
- rewrite intellectual-sounding claims into statements that could fail
- prefer smaller clean definitions over grand totalizing labels

## Common Misuse

- treating category words as explanations
- stacking abstractions until the object disappears
- calling something fundamental merely because it sounds deep
- mistaking a beautiful phrase for a disciplined decomposition

## Example Translation

AGI is not a primitive. It is a bundle term. A first-principles move defines performance breadth, transfer, autonomy, reliability, cost, and deployment boundaries before asking about timelines.

## What This Tradition Corrects

- label worship
- category confusion
- verbal certainty without structural clarity
- grand abstractions that hide mixed dimensions
- explanation by prestige vocabulary
- premature argument before object definition

## Translation Patterns

- Turn nouns into dimensions.
- Turn explanation into mechanism.
- Turn fluency into falsifiable claims.
- Turn category labels into boundary choices.
- Turn conceptual disagreement into definitional work.
- Turn “deep” language into cleaner primitives.

## Failure Signals

- The answer sounds profound but the key term remains undefined.
- A bundled concept is still being treated as a single variable.
- The imported framework adds prestige but no sharper distinctions.
- Readers leave with better vocabulary but no cleaner object.
- Competing claims still cannot disagree on the same thing.
- The output cannot say what would falsify the explanation.

## What To Pull Forward Operationally

- A stricter definition of the core noun.
- One dimension split that clarifies the object.
- One sentence that distinguishes explanation from redescription.
- One claim that can be translated into observable form.
- One prompt rewrite that removes hidden conclusions from the term itself.
